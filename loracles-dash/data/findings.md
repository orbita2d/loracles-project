## Headline

**On the paper's recipe (v2), subliminal transfer works for four of five animals, and the LoRAcle can read it for three of them.**

- **v2 behaviour.** Using the authors' 50-question first-word metric at the final adapter: **owl 75%** (base 5%, control 7%), **butterfly 71%** (2% / 2%), **tiger 44%** (5% / 1%), **whale 42%** (3% / 3%). Dolphin, the animal the paper shows reaching ~98%, stays at **2.6%** (base 0.6%, control 1.6%). We match or beat the authors' released LoRAs for owl, butterfly and tiger, are below them for whale, and fail for dolphin.
- **v2 LoRAcle.** At the final adapter it names the student's animal for **butterfly 98–100%**, **tiger 54–60%** and **dolphin 18% (correct V) / 74% (buggy V)** of rollouts. It also catches **whale at step 10k (71% / 90%)** but mostly loses it by the end (1% / 30%). It **misses owl entirely** (≤2.5%) despite owl having the strongest behaviour. The control is never read as any animal (0/320). So LoRAcle readability doesn't track behavioural strength: dolphin is readable but barely expressed, owl is expressed but unreadable.
- **v1 vs v2.** v1 (10k examples, 15 epochs, lr decayed to 0) transferred weakly (owl 6.5% → 11.6%, butterfly 2.2% → 7.9%, tiger 3.7% → 7.3% vs control; dolphin and whale nothing). The LoRAcle read every v1 student as "number sequences", except whale under buggy V (8/80). The v2 changes, about 3.5× more data, ~2 epochs by step 10k and lr 1e-4 cosine, account for the gap, as the *Paper gap* write-up predicted.
- **J-lens.** For the v1 students, decoding the mean WikiText activation shift through the J-lens names the trained animal for all five, dolphin and whale included (rank 3–29 of ~40k word tokens at layer 30), while the control and the paper's owl decode to junk. So the trait was present in activations even when behaviour and the LoRAcle missed it. A held-out check (choose layers on WikiText validation, score on WikiText test) picks layer 32 alone (+0.32 nats vs +0.25 at layer 30); averaging layers doesn't help.
- **LoRAcle on public LoRAs.** On 40 LoRAs with known behaviour it reads the training-data topic and stated opinions, but not properties of the training signal: emergent misalignment, reward hacking, backdoor triggers, sandbagging, or false facts. See *Public LoRAs*.
